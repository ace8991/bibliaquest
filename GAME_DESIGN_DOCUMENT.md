# Game design — playable Missions 01–02

Vision: third-person biblical adventure, Haitian Creole first. Current menus and narrative also support French, English and Spanish. Windows first; mobile remains future feasibility work.

The player is an unnamed observer and companion. David remains the protagonist and eventual victor. This build is a short foundation, not the eventual polished 15–20 minute chapter.

## Playable flow

Mission 01: blue-banner camp soldier → three dialogue pages → five-stone teaching question → 25 XP.

Mission 02: red-banner lookout → learn about the armies, challenge and fear → blue-tent veteran → learn about the forty-day challenge → four-answer quiz → 35 XP. The veteran cannot unlock the quiz before the lookout conversation is completed. Leaving a conversation early allows a later restart and does not count as completing it. Both missions can be saved and resumed. Language changes do not alter progress.

The first question previews a later scriptural event; it does not let the player gather David's stones. Mission 02 presents the challenge through fictional educational soldier conversations, not a voiced Goliath performance. Combat, Goliath's model and cinematics are absent. Missions 03–05 remain future work. Chapter completion stays false.

## World and navigation

Walkable rolling ground, an incised dry riverbed, a central path with branches to the new soldiers, leveled tent sites, supply areas and a fire ring. Blue and red banners, translated nameplates and a target distance help navigation. Opposing tents sit beyond the riverbed. A terrain-following stone wall bounds exploration. Placement and materials are illustrative, not archaeological claims.

## Presentation

Warm earth, blue/red identification colors, charcoal UI and gold accents. No timed questions or penalties for wrong answers. Text is original educational paraphrase in four languages and requires native-speaker review. The world combines original generated geometry with verified CC0 scanned surface maps, contact shadows and atmospheric haze. Characters carry illustrative shields/spears but have no combat behavior. The player and soldiers are skinned human models with skeletal idle, walk, run, jump and guard animation (hand-authored, no motion capture); see "Characters" below. Voice acting, music, environmental audio, facial animation and production cinematics remain pending.

## Updated environmental presentation

Broad rolling hills and a photographic cloudy sky give the camp more open space. Dusty paths, coarser soil, scattered scanned rocks, patches of vegetation, matte pots and worn tent hems provide varied scale and surface detail. The flora/props are artistic choices, not assertions about the exact historical site. Gameplay and the observer's role are unchanged.

## Characters

Four people are visible: the observer (the player's fictional companion figure, a young man in a sand-coloured tunic — never David) and three fictional soldiers who act as educational guides. Each soldier has his own build, face, beard and tunic colour so the player can tell them apart at a distance: a broad camp soldier in olive with a bronze cap, a lean lookout in red-brown with a leather cap, and an older grey-bearded veteran in blue-grey. All wear knee-length belted tunics and sandals; soldiers stand guard with a spear and a round shield and have no combat behaviour. These costume and equipment choices are an artistic interpretation of the period, not a proven historical reconstruction. The characters aim to be credible and respectful, not photorealistic. Their faces do not yet animate during dialogue.
